Q: What chronological patterns in time do you feel most directly? Kairological?

My first introduction to Chronos and Kairos was from Jenny Odell's Saving Time. In the book's introduction, she describes chronos as the linear time that is constantly and relentlessly moving forward while kairos, as Brand also writes in The Clock of The Long Now, is the "opportune moment". I see chronos as a personal and selfish measure of time, almost microscopic, while kairos is the bigger picture of time, you can really only see clearly once you have stepped away.

Chronos: I've always though of chronology as "order" and "progress", something positive and to seek from day-to-day life in effort to move forward towards the "right path". But something about it also feels prescriptive and rigid. I can only move onto to the next task or event once I have completed the prerequisite. In this sense, my field of view is turned inwards and self centered. I am measuring my steps in this moment, perhaps in preparation for a tomorrow. But chronology is a pattern that has and still exists outside of the human. It is something that nature already abides by (seasons, life cycles, etc...) regardless of human intervention.

Kairos: I imagine as "the right time" that we need to be aware in order to seize it. It requires to be fully present to recognize it, but to also be equipped to take action when the moment arises. I think there is also an element of "missed opportunities" when looking through a kairological lens, as it is not something that can be forced or manufactured. I think of my move from San Francisco to New York as a kairological moment - the world was a shit show in 2020-2021 and the COVID19 pandemic caused many unexpected changes in my life, and I was presented the option to pack up my life and move to a new city.

Making: A Sundial

My inspiration for this with this assignment was to create a texted based sundial that did not tell the exact hour of day, but rather a general sense of moment during the day. Especially during my time at ITP, I find that I am chasing hours and minutes, and each time I check the time, I am surprised by the numbers that are shown to me. I ended up creating a very scrappy and simple sundail by laser cutting "sun benchmarks" onto a sheet paper and gluing it to a platform to maintain the domed/arched shape. As a light source, presumably the sun (but in this case it is my phone flashlight), moves across the sky, the light that shines through the cutouts create shadows that say "sunrise", "am", "noon", "pm", and "sunset".
