Q: How are different art forms (sculpture, visual arts, literature, theater, music, film, etc) situated in time?

Something that was mentioned in the Mindscape podcast was the visualizing time in the form of a circle. Which can take a lot of forms, curved loops, spirals, maybe even "Jeremy Bearimy". I especially enjoyed the thought that "we are looking at a small part of a really big circle" and then toying with the idea of "seeing a lot of lines that connect into a circle". Our immediate perception of time is so limited, I mean look how long it took for us to realize that Earth was not the center of the galaxy, and that our galaxy wasn't the only one in the universe.

I believe, also, that regardless of the medium, art tends to seek distinction between time periods - past vs present vs future, important vs mundane. For example, to distinguish past from present, some mediums will use color treatment or textural changes to distinguish the differences. Making: A Time Machine

I approached the assignment with the expectation to build upon it further later in the semester, so this experimentation is focusing on getting an understanding of programming for time without delay. My project this week is a reflection of my interest in time keeping - not in the way of telling and tracking time, but rather the feedback of the passage of time. This "device" is made of 2 vibe motors pulsing at different rates. Thats it. (of course, there is a microcontroller involved, and its all held together on a half size breadboard)

What I am hoping to explore with this project is the haptic experience of feeling two rhythms in an asynchronous relationship. One rhythm that stays constant and is fixed to a "master" clock (hate how oppressive this language is) which I might connect to an external source via wifi or bluetooth to keep "accurate". The other rhythm that is adjustable at predesignated intervals. I don't have a concrete guideline for the intervals - might be the ways different cultures divide up time. I imagine this to be a handheld device or and object you can rest your hand on to feel the tactile feedback - in a meditative and reflective way.


I do feel the need to preface that I did not spend too much time on the code logic, so I will not pretend to understand it. I had to ask around to see how others were able to achieve a continous loop for their output and the logic did not quite track for me. I might have to spend a little bit more time next week to absorb and digest it more (maybe book some coding lab office hours, too...)
