an interactive installation to (re)connect with something that was once lost

What do you expect when you pick up the receiver of an old landline telephone?

Does your hand remember a number to dial before your mind?
Does the intimacy of this gesture carry you somewhere else?
Perhaps another place, another time, or another voice?

Pick up the phone.
Feel the weight of it in your hand, and pressed against your ear.
Offer something true: a memory, a loss, a goodbye you never finished saying. In the same spot, at another moment in time, an unknown listener will become the keeper of your words.

This work is rooted in a childhood shaped by routines of goodbyes — between countries, between parents, between languages. In those in-between spaces, my stories were carried across distance and shared with strangers never to be met again.

So now I ask you, what does it mean to be witnessed by someone you may never know, and to carry the weight of a voice that trusted the silence enough to speak?
